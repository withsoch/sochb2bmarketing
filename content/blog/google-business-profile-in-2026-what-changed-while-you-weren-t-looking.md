Most advice about your Google Business Profile starts with "just keep it updated." Post a photo. Reply to reviews. Fill in your hours. That advice is not wrong. It is just based on a version of GBP that no longer fully exists, and the gap between what you think is there and what Google has actually built matters, because a buyer who Googles your company before replying to your cold outreach is not seeing what you expect.

Google Business Profile has changed more in the past twelve months than in the several years before that. Four shifts account for almost everything: the Q&A section is gone and a Gemini-powered replacement is already drawing answers from your profile; review enforcement was overhauled twice in April 2026; freshness signals now reward a pattern of activity, not a quarterly tidy-up; and AI Overviews have added a new impression surface where your accuracy either helps or quietly hurts you.

What follows is a fast account of each change and what it costs you to ignore it.

## What exactly changed on Google Business Profile and why it matters now

Four material changes define the current state of Google Business Profile: [the Q&A API was discontinued in November 2025 and replaced by the Gemini-powered Ask Maps feature](https://www.exploredigital.com/blog/top-8-biggest-changes-to-google-business-profile-in-2026-so-far/); freshness signals now require consistent activity rather than one-off edits; Google overhauled its review policies twice in April 2026, explicitly banning staff-name prompts and employee quotas; and AI Overviews have split the visibility surface so that your profile accuracy now determines whether you appear in three different places rather than one. For a B2B company, each of these changes affects the first impression a buyer forms before they ever speak to you.

## Q&A is gone and Ask Maps has taken its place

The Q&A API was officially discontinued on 3 November 2025, with the public-facing Q&A section beginning deprecation on 3 December 2025. The sequence is finished. There is no longer a public place on your profile where you write an answer and a customer reads it as you wrote it.

![Woman typing on a laptop at a desk with a notebook beside her](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7793180/pexels-photo-7793180.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fm=webp&fit=crop&w=1600&h=900)

*With the Q&A section gone, your website and profile content now become the raw material an AI assistant reads on your behalf.*

The feature is being replaced by Ask Maps, an AI-powered tool that uses Google's Gemini AI to answer customer questions automatically, pulling from your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and your website content. The practical consequence for a B2B firm is that you have lost your one channel for placing controlled, specific answers about how your service works.

### What Ask Maps pulls from instead

Google's AI, powered by Gemini, generates answers using information from your Business Profile, including hours, amenities, reviews, and photos. If the AI finds the answer clearly in those sources, it will display it directly on the results page. If the information is missing or inconsistent, Gemini will either skip the answer or infer it from user content, which can lead to errors.

Google describes Ask Maps as combining "Gemini model capabilities together with the world's freshest map." Customers can suggest edits conversationally, right from Ask Maps, and share what Google calls an insider tip. Google's own example is someone saying "more parking is available behind the building," which Ask Maps will then surface to others. Information about your business can now be added by people who do not work for you, through a conversation you never see. A tip that was true last year, or true of the business next door, can end up attached to you.

### How to give Google accurate material before it guesses

Your only real control is making sure the content Ask Maps pulls from is accurate and complete. Businesses with thin websites, unanswered reviews, or incomplete profiles are the most likely to get misrepresented by AI-generated answers. For a B2B service firm, that means your services section, your business description, and the FAQ content on your website need to say, plainly, what you do, who you do it for, and how the process works. Gemini will fill the silence if you do not.

## The new review rules: what is now explicitly banned

Two separate but connected events happened in less than 48 hours. On 16 April 2026, Google published its 2025 Trust and Safety Report alongside three new Maps protections. On 16 and 17 April 2026, Google officially implemented the new pre-publication screening tools and updated its Maps Rating Manipulation clauses regarding staff name requests. Most coverage picks up only the staff-name clause. Both changes matter.

![Man looking at his phone screen while sitting in an office setting](https://images.pexels.com/photos/9052860/pexels-photo-9052860.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fm=webp&fit=crop&w=1600&h=900)

*A plain, uncoached request for feedback is still allowed, it is the scripted coaching that now breaks the rules.*

Google's 2025 Trust and Safety Report shows [292 million policy-violating reviews blocked or removed](https://launchcodex.com/blog/seo-geo-ai/google-business-profile-review-policy-update/) last year. Enforcement is active and automated.

**Banned practices under the updated Rating Manipulation policy:**

- **Staff-name prompts.** You cannot ask a customer to mention a specific team member by name; Google's Trust and Safety systems now flag this pattern automatically.
- **Employee review quotas.** Directing employees to hit specific review targets is now a named violation. Soliciting reviews that mention employee names or specific content is banned.
- **On-premise pressure.** Tablets at the counter, QR codes handed over face-to-face while an employee waits, and "review us before you leave" prompts are all out.
- **Review gating.** Sending review requests only to customers you expect to respond positively, while skipping customers who had a poor experience, is gating and the practice itself is the problem.
- **Incentivised requests.** Incentivising reviews with discounts, gifts, or loyalty points is prohibited, including offering incentives to revise or remove a negative review.
- **AI-generated review content.** Google explicitly prohibits AI-generated review content. If a customer uses a generative AI tool to write a review, the review violates policy even if the underlying experience was real.

**What a compliant review request looks like:** a plain, post-project message that asks the client to share their experience, sent after the work is complete, with no instruction on tone, named staff, or specific phrases. The key requirement is authenticity, using neutral review requests that do not encourage customers to mention specific employees or provide only positive feedback. B2B founders sometimes react to this by stopping review requests altogether. That is the wrong correction. The ask itself is fine. The coaching is what is banned. ## Freshness is now a pattern, not a one-off task

Many guides describe a "30-day rule" as if one monthly post resets a timer. That framing is too simple. Google looks for a consistent pattern of activity over time, and a service business that posts once a quarter and then scrambles is not producing that signal.

![Small team gathered around a whiteboard discussing notes in an office](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7698712/pexels-photo-7698712.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fm=webp&fit=crop&w=1600&h=900)

*Freshness now means a steady rhythm of real updates, not a single scramble once a quarter.*

Multiple local SEO reports confirm that profiles inactive for 30 or more days experience measurable drops in impressions. For a B2B firm with no physical footfall, the challenge is working out what to post. The answer is not to manufacture content. It is to treat your profile as a record of your working life.

### What counts as a freshness signal for a service business

- **Service updates.** If you have added a new service line, a new market you cover, or a new delivery format, that goes on your profile as an update, and into your services section.
- **Team photos.** A new hire, a team day, a training certification. These are factual and they signal that the business is active without requiring invented copy.
- **Project milestones.** A completed engagement, a signed partnership, an event you spoke at. You do not need client names to post that work happened.
- **Profile field reviews.** Checking and correcting your NAP (name, address, phone) consistency, your business category, and your attributes counts as meaningful maintenance and keeps your data clean for Gemini to pull from.

Ask Maps re-reads your content continuously. A website update, a new service, or a batch of new reviews changes what it says, so this needs to be a habit, not a single afternoon project.

## Verification and suspension: the risks most businesses are walking into

Google is suspending Business Profiles at record rates in 2026. The pattern most likely to catch a B2B company is not an obvious policy breach. It is a recent rebrand, an office move, or a round of optimisation edits made too quickly.

**Common suspension triggers in the current enforcement climate:**

- **Keyword-stuffed business names.** Appending service keywords or location descriptors to your business name is now an instant suspension trigger. Your profile name should match your registered trading name and nothing more.
- **Rapid multi-field edits.** A burst of quick edits trips Google's automated review. If you have recently rebranded or moved premises, spread your profile updates over several sessions rather than making every change in one sitting.
- **Address and category mismatches.** Misclassification is a top suspension cause: a business listing a shared virtual office as a storefront will be suspended, while one correctly configured as service-area from a home base rarely is.
- **Community-suggested edits.** Someone suggesting an incorrect edit to your hours or category can trigger a suspension if enough users corroborate it. Turn on email notifications for profile changes and check your dashboard weekly.

If you are suspended, do not create a new listing, as that compounds the problem. File a reinstatement through the official form with proof of operation. Check Google's [verification guidance](https://support.google.com/business/thread/423749025/april-2026-%F0%9F%8C%B8-your-spring-checklist?hl=en) for the current documentation requirements before you submit.

## How AI Overviews changed the visibility equation

Your Google Business Profile now feeds three distinct surfaces: the classic local panel in Maps, the Maps AI summary card powered by Ask Maps, and [AI Overviews in the organic search results](https://biziq.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics/). Each draws on your profile data. Each can misrepresent you if your information is thin or inconsistent.

AI search has shifted traditional metrics. Impressions have dropped, but qualified actions have held steady. The right things to measure now are answer presence and local engagement, not raw impression counts. For a B2B company whose buyers are not walking in from the street, this matters in a specific way: the buyer searching "[your company name] + reviews" or "[your service] + [your city]" before responding to your outreach is encountering all three of these surfaces at once. What they see in the first ten seconds is shaped almost entirely by your profile accuracy, your recent posts, and the reviews you have collected.

NAP consistency, meaning your name, address, and phone number matching exactly across your website, your profile, and any directory listings, feeds directly into how confidently Google surfaces you in AI-generated results. An inconsistency that seemed harmless when it was only about ranking is now an input to an AI answer that a buyer reads without clicking through.

## FAQ

### What are the biggest changes to Google Business Profile right now?

Google has transitioned to its new AI-powered Ask Maps experience, meaning users increasingly receive AI-generated answers instead of browsing business-written Q&A. Alongside that, the April 2026 review policy overhaul added explicit bans on staff-name prompts and employee quotas, and freshness signals now require consistent ongoing activity rather than periodic updates.

### What are the new Google review rules and what is now banned?

In April 2026, Google expanded its review policies by explicitly prohibiting review quotas for employees and requests that ask customers to mention specific staff members in reviews. Review gating, on-premise pressure tactics, incentivised requests, and AI-generated review content are also prohibited. A compliant ask is a neutral, post-project message with no coaching on content or tone.

### What replaced the Q&A section on Google Business Profile?

Google replaced the Q&A section with Ask Maps, a Gemini-powered AI feature that launched publicly on 12 March 2026. You can no longer manually post an answer the way you used to. Your only real control is making sure the content Ask Maps pulls from is accurate and complete.
