## Why Google Reviews Work Differently for B2B Companies

B2B companies find it harder to accumulate Google reviews not because their clients are unwilling, but because the sales cycle is longer, decisions involve multiple people, and satisfied clients rarely think to leave public feedback unprompted. Review velocity, the steady accumulation of reviews over time, matters more than any single burst of requests, and the B2B relationship structure makes that consistency genuinely difficult to build without a process behind it.

A consumer business completes dozens of transactions a week and can ask at the point of purchase. A B2B service firm, whether an accounting practice, an IT support company or a management consultancy, might complete a handful of meaningful engagements per quarter. Each one represents a real opportunity, but the window for asking closes quickly once the project wraps and both sides move on.

The consequence is a thin, static profile. Prospects searching for your firm will find it. What they find when they get there, one review from two years ago or none at all, is what shapes their first impression. The answer is not to send more emails. It is to tie the review request to the moments in your client relationship that already exist.

## Step 1: Claim and Optimise Your Google Business Profile Before You Ask for Anything

Every review request you send points somewhere. If your Google Business Profile is unclaimed, incomplete or showing the wrong address, you are sending clients to a page that undermines the credibility the review was meant to build. Fixing the profile is not optional preparation. It is the first step.

![Woman at a desk reviewing a business listing profile on a laptop screen](/blog/why-b2b-clients-dont-leave-google-reviews-inline-1.jpg)

*A profile that is claimed, accurate and easy to find is the foundation every review request depends on.*

Check three things before you do anything else.

- **NAP consistency:** Your business name, address and phone number must match exactly across your website, your profile and any directory listings. Inconsistencies erode trust in local search rankings.
- **Category selection:** Choose the most specific primary category available for your type of firm. An IT support company listed under a generic technology category is harder for the right buyer to find.
- **Your review link:** Inside the Google Business Profile dashboard, there is a shortcut to generate a direct review link. Copy it, save it and use it in every request. Clients should never have to search for where to leave a review.

B2B firms skip profile verification more often than any other business type, usually because no one has been assigned to own it. ## Step 2: Build the Ask Into Your Client Delivery Process

Most B2B firms that want more Google reviews treat the ask as a one-off campaign. They send a batch email, get a few responses, and the number stalls again. The problem is not the email. It is the timing.

![Two colleagues shaking hands across a table at the end of a meeting](/blog/why-b2b-clients-dont-leave-google-reviews-inline-2.jpg)

*The best moment to ask for a review is right after the client has seen the finished work, not weeks later.*

### Identifying the right moment in the B2B relationship

The moment that works is the post-delivery handoff: the call or message that happens when a project closes, a quarterly review wraps up, or a report is delivered and approved. The client has just seen the outcome of the work. Their satisfaction is at its peak and the relationship is active. That is the window.

Generic follow-up sequences sent weeks later miss it. By then, the client has moved on to other priorities. The ask feels like an afterthought rather than a natural extension of a good working relationship.

### Who on your team should send the ask and how

The request should come from the person who managed the relationship, not from a marketing email address. For a small firm, that is usually the owner or the account lead. For a larger team, assign it as a step in the project close checklist so it does not get skipped.

**Keep the message short and direct.** One sentence acknowledging the work that was completed, one sentence asking for a review, and the direct link. Nothing more. A building supplier might say: "We are glad the fit-out went smoothly. If you have a moment, a short Google review would mean a great deal to us," followed by the link. Clients do not need to be told what to write. They need to be asked clearly and given an easy path to do it.

## Step 3: Make It Effortless, Share Your Direct Review Link Every Time

Generating the link is the easy part. The habit of using it consistently is where most firms fall short. Once you have your Google review shortlink from the GBP dashboard, put it everywhere a client might interact with your business.

- **Email signature:** A short line at the bottom of every outgoing email, visible to every client contact throughout the engagement.
- **Invoice footer:** Clients open invoices. A brief line next to your contact details costs nothing and keeps the ask visible without being intrusive.
- **LinkedIn messages:** When a project closes and you are already exchanging messages on LinkedIn, include the link there. It meets the client where the conversation is already happening.
- **Onboarding documents:** Mention early that you value client feedback. It primes the expectation and makes the end-of-project ask feel consistent rather than unexpected.

The goal is to remove every point of friction. A client who has to search for your business, find the reviews tab and navigate the submission form is a client who will not complete it. One click to the review form is the standard to aim for.

## Step 4: Respond to Every Review to Signal That Feedback Is Valued

Replying to reviews is not just courtesy. It is a signal to every future client who reads your profile that feedback matters to your firm and that someone is paying attention. A profile with twenty reviews and zero replies looks abandoned. A profile with eight reviews and a thoughtful response to each one looks like an active, accountable business.

Replies should be conversational and specific to what the reviewer said. A generic "Thank you for your kind words" repeated across every review tells the next reader nothing. Reference the type of work, the outcome, or the aspect of the relationship the client mentioned. That specificity also carries keyword value for local search without requiring any manipulation.

Negative reviews deserve the same attention. A measured, professional reply to a critical review demonstrates more credibility than a page of five-star responses alone. It shows prospective clients how you handle difficulty, which in a B2B context is often exactly what they are trying to assess. Google's own [guidance on review replies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) makes clear that responses should be helpful and non-promotional. ## What to Avoid: Google's Rules on Review Requests

Asking clients for reviews is permitted and encouraged. Several common tactics are not.

According to [Google's content policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en), offering incentives in exchange for reviews, whether discounts, gifts, or any other benefit, is prohibited and classified as fake and misleading content. Asking only for positive reviews, or setting up a pre-screening step that filters unhappy clients before they reach the review form, also violates the policy. Any of these can result in reviews being removed or a profile being penalised.

The straightforward rule: ask all clients, ask genuinely and let the review reflect their honest experience. A B2B firm with a strong service record does not need to engineer the outcome.

## FAQ

### Why is it harder for B2B companies to get Google reviews than B2C businesses?

B2B firms complete fewer transactions, involve more decision-makers per client and have longer gaps between natural interaction points. There is no post-purchase moment to prompt a review automatically. Without a deliberate process tied to the client lifecycle, the request simply never gets made.

### Can you ask clients directly for a Google review without violating Google's policies?

Yes. Google explicitly supports asking clients for reviews. What the policy prohibits is incentivising reviews with discounts or gifts, asking only for positive reviews, or filtering out unhappy clients before they reach the review form. A direct, honest ask is both permitted and encouraged.

### What is the best time to ask a B2B client for a Google review?

The post-delivery handoff is the most effective moment: immediately after a project sign-off, a quarterly review call or a report approval. The client has just seen the outcome of the work, the relationship is active and their satisfaction is at its highest point in the engagement.

## Start With the Process, Not the Campaign

The firms that accumulate Google reviews consistently are not the ones who run the most aggressive campaigns. They are the ones who made the ask a standard part of how they close work, put the review link somewhere every client will see it and replied to every piece of feedback that came in.

Review velocity compounds. A few genuine reviews added steadily across a year builds a profile that earns trust before a buyer has spoken to anyone at your firm. That is what a credible Google presence actually looks like.
