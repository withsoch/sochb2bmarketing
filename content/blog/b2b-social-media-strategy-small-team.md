Advice on B2B social media strategy usually start with how often to post and which formats to try. That is the second question, not the first.

Before anyone replies to your email or accepts your invite, they open your LinkedIn profile, your company page and your Google listing. They read the last few posts and the reviews. In a small market like Estonia, they may also ask a mutual contact.

So the order matters: make the presence credible first, then give it a steady rhythm, then measure it. Volume without the first step just gives sceptical buyers more to doubt.

## What is a B2B social media strategy?

A B2B social media strategy is a plan for how a business uses social platforms to earn trust with the people involved in a business purchase. It covers which platforms you use, what you publish, who speaks, and what a buyer finds when they check you out before making contact.

The difference from B2C is the buyer. B2B purchases involve several decision-makers, which leads to longer decision cycles. You are not persuading one person in one sitting.

"B2B in social media" simply means selling to other businesses on platforms such as LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X and YouTube. "B2B strategies" in this context are the choices that make a long, committee-led decision easier for you to win.

## Why do buyers check your social profiles before they reply?

Because they research independently. [B2B buyers compare competitors and read executive posts on social media](https://worldmetrics.org/b2b-social-media-statistics/), and they often decide before any sales conversation begins.

![Person at a desk reading a company page on a laptop](/blog/b2b-social-media-strategy-small-team-inline-1.jpg)

*Long before any sales call, a buyer is quietly reading what your profiles say about you.*

The sales cycle is long too. [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/b2b-social-media/) puts [the average B2B sale at 272 days](https://nealschaffer.com/b2b-social-media-marketing/), so the job is building trust rather than chasing quick conversions. Every profile visit during those months is a small test.

Marketers see the value. In Wpromote research cited by [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/b2b-social-media-marketing/), [60% of B2B marketers polled in 2023 called social the most effective channel for revenue](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/b2b-social-media-statistics/). But buyers only give that value to profiles that look alive and accurate.

Picture an accounting practice with a founder profile that still shows a job from three years ago, a company page with no posts since spring, and a Google listing with unanswered reviews. Nothing there is dramatic, but every item says "nobody is looking after this".

## Which platforms should a B2B company prioritise?

For a small team, pick in this order.

- **LinkedIn first.** It is where buying committees check people and companies. Keep the company page complete and let the founder and two or three colleagues carry most of the voice. Our [LinkedIn and lead generation](https://www.withsocialcatalyst.com/services#linkedin-leadgen) work starts here.
- **Google Business Profile second.** It is the local trust check: opening hours, services, photos and reviews. For an IT support firm or building supplier, it is often the first thing a buyer sees.
- **Instagram as proof.** Use it for the work, the team and the site or office. It supports credibility rather than generating enquiries.

Then skip things deliberately. Do not open a TikTok account, a second LinkedIn page or a fourth channel because a checklist said so. An abandoned profile does more damage than a missing one.

Estonian audiences are small and buyers often know each other. Reputation travels by word of mouth, so a consistent, honest presence in two places beats a thin one in five.

## What should a B2B company actually post?

Aim for a weekly mix, not a content calendar full of ideas nobody has time to produce. A workable week looks like this:

- **One expertise post.** A real question a client asked, answered in plain language.
- **One behind-the-scenes post.** A job in progress, a process, a workspace.
- **One people post.** A colleague, what they do and what they are good at.
- **One proof post.** A finished project, a client comment you have permission to use, a review.
- **One third-party share.** Useful industry content with a line of your own opinion on top.

The practical problem is raw material, not ideas. The people doing the work are rarely the people writing the posts.

The fix is a fifteen-minute call or a shared chat once a week. Someone asks what happened on site or with clients, collects photos and voice notes, and turns them into posts in one batch. Owners do not need to write; they need to talk and approve.

### Founder and employee voices vs the brand page

People read people. Employee advocacy works because a colleague's post reads as a person, not an advert. The company page should hold the facts and repost the best material, while individuals do the talking.

### What the rules mean in practice

The 5-5-5 and rule-of-7 ideas are covered in the FAQ below. In practice, both say the same thing: small, repeated signals beat occasional big pushes.

## What happens after a buyer finds you?

This is the conversion layer, and most strategies skip it. Walk through your own profiles as a stranger would and check each of these:

![Hand holding a smartphone above a desk with a laptop nearby](/blog/b2b-social-media-strategy-small-team-inline-3.jpg)

*Check your profiles as a stranger would: one clear bio, recent replies and a single next step.*

- **Bio and headline.** Does it say who you help and with what, in one line?
- **Pinned post.** Does something at the top explain your offer or show your best proof?
- **Reviews.** Are recent ones answered, politely, by a named person?
- **Comments and DMs.** Does anyone reply within a working day?
- **Next step.** Is there one clear way to get in touch, the same on every profile?

Fixing these takes an afternoon. It also changes what every future post is worth, because more of the people it reaches will find something solid when they look.

## How do you measure a B2B social media strategy?

Judge it by business signals, not platform counts. Last-click attribution will mislead you, because a buyer may look at you for months and then come through a direct email or a referral.

Keep to a short set:

- **Qualified conversations.** How many started with "I saw your post" or "I looked at your profile"?
- **Profile visits from target companies.** LinkedIn shows who looked; note the ones you want.
- **Inbound mentions of content.** Clients or prospects quoting a post back to you.

Review these monthly, with sales in the room. Likes and follower totals can sit in the background.

## FAQ

### What is the 5-5-5 rule on social media?

It is a routine heuristic, and versions differ. Most describe a short daily habit of engaging with a handful of posts, accounts and comments. The useful idea is a small, fixed engagement habit rather than occasional bursts. It is not a law and has no proven formula.

### What is the rule of 7 in B2B?

It is the marketing idea that a buyer needs around seven touchpoints before acting. Treat it as a reminder that trust builds through repetition, not as a measured threshold. With committees and long cycles, the real number varies widely.

### What is B2B in social media?

It means using social platforms such as LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube or X to reach other businesses, buyers and decision-makers, rather than consumers. The content is usually more expert-led, the audience smaller, and the aim is building trust over a long decision.
